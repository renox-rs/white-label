# construction

The vertical app for fit-out and construction subcontractors ([spec](../../docs/verticals/02-construction.md)):
the project, its termination mid-way and the settlement of its final account, on top of the shared
`wl-*` modules. Construction reaches the other modules only through the contracts of `wl-kernel`
(attendance, purchasing and stock, tickets, notifications, invoices), never through their crates.

The host mounts it when `client.toml` names `app = "construction"`:

```
use renox::prelude::*;

# let _ =
App::new().module(construction::Construction)
# ;
```

What is here so far:

| Part | Spec | Module |

| The Project with its machine: `setup`, `in_progress`, `on_hold`, `practical_completion`, `terminated`, `closed`, `cancelled`; `start` (needs a budget), `hold`, `resume`, `terminate`, `settle_final_account` and `cancel` are built (`complete` and `close` come with the practical completion and the defects liability period) | §6, §7.1 *Project* | `project` |
| `terminate` (`CON.ACL.15`): the project manager requests it with the reason (terminated by the client, our default, main contractor insolvent, agreed), the termination notice and the effective date, which may be in the past; the kernel defers it as an approval request for the MD (never the requester) and the project keeps its state until then. At the approval the project is `terminated` (not an end), both retention parts are made due unless `retention_due_on_termination` is off (`⚠ open: lawyer (L-37)`) and the final account IPA is made: a `draft` IPA of the project becomes it, valued to the effective date, an IPA still `submitted` stays. After the commit: attendance for a day after the effective date is refused (the attendance module asks `ContextClosure`) and attendance already recorded after it is flagged for the reviewer; the project manager gets a task to reassign the crews; open purchase requests with no order are cancelled; procurement gets one task listing the open orders to cancel or short-close (nothing is cancelled for the supplier); a stock count of the site store is opened; the accountant gets one task per `active` guarantee; open snag and defect tickets are cancelled with the reason; the client contacts are e-mailed; `ProjectTerminated` is emitted | §3.3, §4.8, §7.1 `terminate`, `CON.ACL.15` | `termination` |
| `settle_final_account`: a terminated project closes when its final account IPA is certified (the client's certificate, or a court or arbitration decision recorded as the certificate) and invoiced, or its invoice is written off; open retention parts, guarantees and a stock count still open are warnings for the closing screen, not guards | §4.8, §7.1 `settle_final_account` | `final_account` |
| `cancel` (the MD, with a reason): only from `setup`, and refused once attendance, a goods receipt or an IPA exists; the open purchase orders are flagged for cancellation with one task for procurement | §7.1 `cancel` | `cancellation` |
| `award::accept` (the step a quotation's `accept` ends with): the project made in `setup` from an award with its document; for a blocked client `BlockGuard::check(client, start)` refuses it with the text the viewer may see (neutral *on hold* by default) and writes nothing. The block effect of the kind `project` is `continue`, decided by the MD: running projects keep their attendance, IPAs and variations, nothing is held or terminated by itself, money out to the client (the set-off of a supplier bill from it) waits for an exception approved by compliance in wl-payments, and receipts are never refused | §7.1 *Quotation* `accept`, §7.2 row Project, `PTY.ACL.03` | `award` |
| `submit` and `supersede` of the IPA: the project manager submits a draft that claims something; while an earlier IPA is `submitted` it must be submitted as its replacement (`replaces`, only the most recent pending IPA), which becomes `superseded` in the same transaction and the client contacts are told; two IPAs are never `submitted` at once | §4.4, §7.1 *IPA* | `submission` |
| `certify` and the late certificate: the QS records the cumulative certified work and the certificate, the claim gap (claimed less certified work) is recorded and the drafts' "previous certified" and *previous pending* flag are refreshed; a certificate for a superseded IPA is recorded on the IPA that replaced it, or only attached for reference when that one is certified already (the QS gets a task) | §4.4 step 5, §7.1 *IPA* | `certificate` |
| `CON.RUL.03`: on the cut-off day (25, or the last day of a shorter month) at 02:00 Asia/Dubai every running project without a draft gets one, numbered, with its previous certified (latest `certified` or `invoiced` IPA by number) and marked *previous pending* when an earlier IPA is `submitted`; the QS (and the project manager when pending) get a task; one draft per project per cut-off | §7.2 | `cutoff` |
| The deductions on a certificate (`CON.ACL.14`): the QS records typed lines (`back_charge`, `contra_charge`, `liquidated_damages`, `other` with a description) with the client's reference and the amount excluding VAT on the `submitted` IPA; the project manager accepts or contests each (never a line they recorded), accepting one above AED 25,000 is deferred as an approval request for the MD and the line stays `undecided` until then (a rejection leaves it undecided); `certify` is refused while a line is `undecided`, and the deductions are never netted into the certified work; contested lines are listed with the final account items of the project | §3.3, §4.4 step 5, §6, §7.1 `certify` | `deduction` |
| `invoice` of the IPA (the accountant, `invoices.issue`): the tax invoice for the certified work of the period (never reduced by the deductions, no retention in it); a credit note against it for the accepted liquidated damages and `other` lines; the invoice put in dispute for the contested lines plus their VAT (reason *contested certificate deduction*); the accepted back- and contra-charges recorded as a supplier bill from the client (no purchase order to match) with a task for the accountant to approve it and record the set-off (wl-payments, which moves no money and waits for an exception while the client is blocked); the retention of the period added to the held parts up to the cap. The ids are kept on the IPA as the documents are made, so a run that stopped resumes without making a document twice | §4.4 steps 5-6, §7.1 *IPA* `invoice`, §9 | `invoice`, `retention` |
| `complete` and `close` of the project, as far as the retention needs them: `complete` (`projects.manage`) records the handover certificate and its date, sets the defects liability end date (the days of the project from that date) and makes the first retention part due; `close` needs that period to have passed and makes the final part due. "No open snags" and "no open defect tickets" come with the snag issues | §4.6, §7.1 *Project* | `completion` |
| The release of the retention (`CON.ACL.11`): a part is `held`, then `due` (the first at `complete`, the final at `close` or on the daily run after the defects liability period, both at a termination), then `invoiced` on its own tax invoice at 5% VAT for the remaining amount (the accountant, `invoices.issue`; a terminated project's parts wait for the effective date), then `settled` when that invoice is paid, credited or written off (the kernel's invoice events). A user with `retentions.manage` links a retention bond to a part (a bond below the held amount is a warning) and makes the part due while the bond is `active`. The accountant is told once when a part is due and once when the cap is reached | §4.6, §7.1 *Retention* | `release`, `retention`, `notices` |
| `CON.RUL.07` (daily at 02:00 Asia/Dubai): 30 days before a year after the completion certificate (the termination effective date for a terminated project) the accountant is told that VAT on the retention becomes due, once per project, while a part is `held` or `due`; the accountant may dismiss the notice with a reason, which changes nothing else | §7.2, §9 | `vat_notice` |
| What the client keeps of the retention (`deduct`, `CON.ACL.14`): before a part is invoiced the QS (`retentions.manage`) records the amount with the client's notice or contra-charge reference and the reason; the project manager accepts or contests it (never a deduction they recorded), and accepting above AED 25,000 is deferred as an approval request for the MD. Only an accepted deduction is applied: the part loses the amount (`deduct` on a `held` part, `deduct_due` on a `due` one, `keep` when nothing remains: `kept`), the amount is booked to the project's back-charge cost code through the kernel's cost contract, and no tax document is issued. A contested deduction changes nothing and is listed with the final account items. On an `invoiced` part `deduct` is not offered: the user disputes the retention invoice for the amount and its VAT instead (its resolution `credited` is wl-invoicing's credit note) | §4.6, §7.1 *Retention*, §3.3 | `retention_deduction` |
| The Guarantee (an advance guarantee, a performance bond or a retention bond) with its bank documents: the accountant (`guarantees.manage`) registers it as a `draft` and runs `issue` (the bank document, the amount, the beneficiary and the expiry are set), `reduce` (the bank's amendment or the beneficiary's consent; the advance guarantee not below the unrecovered advance, which the refusal shows), `extend` (the amendment, a later expiry; the reminders restart) and `release` (the returned original or the bank's cancellation letter); neither the amount nor the expiry changes without the document, and each is kept as a document of the guarantee. `CON.RUL.11` (daily at 02:00 Asia/Dubai): 60 days before the expiry of an `active` guarantee whose project has not reached its release point (completion for the advance guarantee, the end of the defects liability period for a bond) the accountant gets a task to ask the bank for an extension and the project manager is notified, once per expiry date. The day after the expiry date the system runs `expire` and the accountant and the project manager are notified. Project `close` gives the accountant one release task per `active` performance or retention bond. | §4.6, §7.1 *Guarantee*, §7.2 | `guarantee`, `expiry` |
| A call on a guarantee (`call`, `settle_call`): the accountant records the beneficiary's demand with the demand notice and the amount (not above the guarantee) and the guarantee is `called`; the MD, the project manager and the accountant are told at once (an urgent notice) and the accountant gets a task to respond. `settle_call` takes the bank's debit advice and the amount paid: a partial call leaves the guarantee `active` for the rest with the same expiry, a call paid in full makes it `released` (a second transition, `settle_call_full`, because a Renox transition has one target), and a call the beneficiary withdrew before payment is settled with amount 0 and the withdrawal letter and leaves it `active` with its full amount. The paid amount is booked to the called-guarantees cost code after the settlement, once, and never before the debit advice. The project manager may mark a call disputed: the paid amount is then listed in the claims of the final account (the closing screen). A `called` guarantee never expires; one back to `active` after its expiry date expires on the next daily run | §4.6, §7.1 *Guarantee*, §9 | `call`, `guarantee` |
| The Variation (the base BL-CON-11, BL-CON-12 and BL-CON-13 need): the QS (`variations.record`) records and prices a draft and records the client's decision; only the project manager (`variations.submit`) submits, resubmits, claims, abandons or withdraws (`CON.ACL.06`). `approve` and `settle_claim` (the agreed amount and the client's reference) add the amount to the contract value, the budget and the next IPA (an omission is stored negative). `reject` (with a reason) leaves the cost of work executed on a site instruction on the variation's own cost code, marked unrecovered, and tells the QS and the project manager; further cost booked counts too. `resubmit` makes revision n+1 in `draft` and keeps the rejected revision read-only, "resubmitted as rev n+1", which can no longer be resubmitted, claimed or abandoned; `claim` needs work executed on a site instruction and lists the variation in the claims of the final account; `abandon` is deferred as an approval request for the MD whatever the amount (`CON.ACL.16`) and writes the cost into the unrecovered variations report. The PDF sent at `submit` is not generated yet | §4.5, §6, §7.1 *Variation*, §3.3 | `variation`, `variation_pages` |
| The IPA, the Retention and the Guarantee with their states | §6, §7.1 | `ipa`, `retention`, `guarantee` |
| The event `ProjectTerminated` | §7.1 | `events` |
| The back-office actions (terminate, closing screen, settle, cancel, complete, close, the retention bond, make due, invoice, the retention deductions, the dispute of the retention invoice, dismissing the VAT notice, the guarantee steps and the call on a guarantee) | §7.1 | `pages` |
